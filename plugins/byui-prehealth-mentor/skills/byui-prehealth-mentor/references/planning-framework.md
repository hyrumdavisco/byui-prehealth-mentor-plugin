# Pre-Health Timeline Planning Framework

Use this framework after identifying the student's professional track and intended entry year.

## 1. Choose the anchor

For pre-med students, anchor the plan to these milestones and work backward:

1. Intended medical-school matriculation
2. Application cycle and desired primary-submission window
3. Latest useful MCAT score-release date
4. Preferred MCAT test window
5. Optional retake window
6. Dedicated or part-time MCAT preparation start
7. Completion of MCAT-relevant coursework

Do not hard-code annual application or score-release dates. Confirm them for the relevant cycle.

For another pre-health track, replace the MCAT milestones with that profession's actual admissions requirements. If the current knowledge files do not cover the track well, provide general planning help and disclose the limitation.

## 2. Test three MCAT windows

Consider three scenarios before recommending one:

- **Earlier/high-buffer:** More time for a retake and an early completed application; may require testing before relevant coursework is complete or while the student is overloaded.
- **Balanced:** Adequate preparation plus a practical retake buffer; usually the default recommendation when the student's readiness supports it.
- **Later/low-buffer:** More initial study time or coursework exposure; increases the chance that a disappointing score delays review or forces a cycle change.

Choose based on readiness and total risk, not merely the earliest possible date.

## 3. Estimate preparation realistically

Ask for a diagnostic score or baseline if available. Estimate preparation from:

- Gap between baseline and target score
- Strength and recency of prerequisite knowledge
- Weekly hours that can actually be protected
- Competing credits, employment, research, service, health, and family responsibilities
- Time reserved for full-length practice exams, review, and recovery

Avoid treating “three months” or any fixed duration as universally sufficient. A plan that requires hours the student cannot sustain is not a plan.

## 4. Apply readiness gates

Before endorsing a test date, check whether the student can reasonably meet these gates:

- Core content has been learned or can be learned before intensive practice
- Weekly study blocks fit the student's real schedule
- Full-length practice exams can be taken under realistic conditions
- Recent practice performance is approaching the target range
- Sleep and workload are stable enough for consistent preparation
- The student understands rescheduling, cancellation, and score-release implications

If several gates are weak, recommend changing the test window, course load, or application cycle rather than relying on motivation alone.

## 5. Build the rest of the application around the anchor

After the exam window is credible, sequence:

- Prerequisite completion and any school-specific courses
- Clinical experience and shadowing
- Service and leadership
- Research, if it fits the student's goals and gaps
- Letters of recommendation
- Personal statement and activity descriptions
- School-list research
- Primary and secondary applications
- Interview preparation
- Financial planning and contingency options

Distinguish a prerequisite from an application-strengthening activity. Prioritize genuine gaps over accumulating more hours in an already strong category.

## 6. Produce a usable plan

The final plan should show:

| Period | Primary milestone | Dependency or evidence | Risk/backup |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current term | Immediate preparation or prerequisite work | Current schedule and baseline | Course-load adjustment if needed |
| Exam-prep period | Study blocks and practice exams | Weekly capacity and score trend | Move test date if readiness gates fail |
| Application period | Materials, letters, and submission | Verified cycle dates | Later cycle or gap-year option |
| Pre-matriculation | Finish degree and meaningful experiences | Admission requirements | Reapplication improvement plan |

Include the student's three next actions and a clear review trigger, such as the first diagnostic, the end of the semester, or six weeks before the test.
