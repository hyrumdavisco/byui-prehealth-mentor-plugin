Common Interview Questions
Resource status — practice prompts, not predictions or scripts. Interview questions and formats vary by school and year; use professional judgment and prepare authentic, respectful responses.


 
Self-Reflection and Solo Practice
	Practice answering questions aloud in front of a mirror or record yourself to analyze your body language, tone, and clarity of responses.
	Utilize Online Resources
	Watch videos and tutorials on YouTube or other educational platforms that discuss common medical school interview questions and provide sample answers. Look for resources that also cover interview etiquette and body language tips.
	Review School-Specific Information
	Familiarize yourself with the mission, values, and unique aspects of each medical school where you have an interview scheduled. Tailor your responses to align with their specific goals and ethos.
	Stay Informed on Current Healthcare Issues
	Stay updated on current healthcare policies, challenges, and advancements. This knowledge can help you engage in discussions about the future of medicine during your interview.
	Practice Time Management
	Practice answering questions concisely and effectively within the typical time frame of an interview response (usually 1-3 minutes per question).
	Seek Feedback and Iterate
	After each practice session, solicit feedback from your peers or mentors on areas for improvement. Use this feedback to refine your answers and overall interview strategy.
	Stay Calm and Confident
	Prior to your interview, practice relaxation techniques to manage nerves. Remember, confidence comes from thorough preparation.
	Prepare Logistically for Virtual Interviews
	If your interview is via video call, ensure your technology works smoothly, your background is professional, and you are well-lit and audible.
	 
By following these steps and dedicating time to thorough preparation, you'll be well-equipped to confidently navigate your medical school interviews.
These instructions outline a comprehensive approach to preparing for both in-person and virtual medical school interviews, emphasizing practice, self-reflection, and leveraging online resources for guidance.

Questions:
Motivation and Personal Qualities:
1. What makes this school a great fit for you?
2. Why do you want to become a doctor?
3. Why did you choose medicine and not another field where you could help others?
4. What are your strengths and weaknesses?
5. With all of the other careers in the healthcare field, what drew you to becoming a physician specifically?
6. What qualities do you have that set you apart from all of the other amazing applicants?
7. What aspects of your life and experiences do you think make you a good candidate for medical school?
8. Tell me why you’re interested in medicine (both what inspired this interest and also what you’ve done to investigate the field and confirm your decision).
Experience and Preparation:
1. Tell me about a time you had a leadership role in your community.
2. Describe your research experience.
3. Where do you see yourself practicing medicine in the future?
4. Describe your clinical experience.
5. What did you learn in undergrad that you think will help you in medical school?
6. What hobbies do you do for fun?
7. Tell us about a challenging experience you had in your life and how you overcame it.
8. Tell me about an experience you’ve had working on a team. What role do you tend to play when working on a team?
9. What has been your most rewarding experience working with others?
Ethical, Policy, and Professionalism:
   1. What will you do if you don’t get accepted to medical school this cycle?
   2. How do you manage your time efficiently?
   3. How do you plan to balance your personal and professional life as a doctor?
   4. What are you looking for in your ideal medical school?
   5. How do you plan to contribute to the diversity of the medical school community?
   6. Do you view healthcare as a right or as a privilege? Explain why for either side.
   7. What skills have you used to cope with stress?
   8. What hesitations or reservations do you have about joining the medical profession?
   9. How do you think the U.S. should address the physician shortage problem, particularly in primary care doctors in rural areas?
   10. What are your thoughts on abortion, euthanasia, and stem cell research? (There is no correct answer here, just be respectful towards the views of others while expressing your views as well).
   11. What is cultural competency? What steps have you taken to become a culturally competent individual?
QUESTIONS AT THE END OF THE INTERVIEW
Insights and Advice:
1. What do you personally think of as the biggest problem in healthcare, and how would you advise my generation of physicians to address it?
2. Looking back on your paths to your professional careers, do you have anything you wish you'd done differently in hindsight?
3. How do you split your time between clinical medicine and your other activities (i.e., research / teaching / community work / business)?
4. Do you have any advice for somebody like me who is interested in pursuing research and clinical medicine?
Specialty and School-Specific:
1. Why did you choose the specialty you work in, and what was an experience that led you to the field? (If your interviewer is/are physicians themselves)
2. What is your favorite part of your specialty you work in?
3. Why did YOU decide to come to [medical school] as a faculty member?
4. What characteristics do you see in successful students at [school]?
5. What is your favorite aspect of the _____ School of Medicine?
6. What do you believe to be the greatest strength of _____ School of Medicine?
7. What were the motivations and inspirations behind _____ change at the medical school?
8. Can you give an example of how the medical school has created a change based on student feedback?
9. What new changes has the school implemented into the curriculum?
10. I heard that you do research in [X]. Could you speak to your experiences in that?
11. I read about [a program in the school] online and was wondering if you knew about it?
12. I heard that the curriculum includes [aspects of the curriculum]. Could you speak to this?
13. Are there any initiatives the school is working on for med students?
DO SCHOOL SPECIFIC questions (osteopathic medical school) 
* Describe briefly your understanding of osteopathic medicine?
* What are the tenets of osteopathic medicine ? (You must know what osteopathic medicine is!!!) 
* How would you explain OMM to an 8 year old ? (Be familiar briefly with what OMM is) 
* What inspired you to apply to a DO school ?
* How would you explain the difference between an MD vs DO to a patient? 
* Have you had experiences with osteopathic medicine or DO physicians before ? 
* How does becoming an osteopathic physician fit into your career goals?
