Each one is well-documented. Each one is avoidable.
01 Top-heavy school lists
Applicants overload their list with highly competitive schools, assuming stats alone will carry them, while ignoring 
that even qualified applicants are regularly denied at top programs.
02 Mission misalignment
Schools have stated missions, focus on things like primary care, rural health, research, or service. If your application 
doesn't reflect a school's mission, even strong stats won't earn an interview. Most applicants don't even check.
03 Geographic blindness
Many public schools admit 90%+ in-state. Applying out-of-state to a heavily in-state-biased school is, statistically, 
like applying to a different planet.

Apply early. Then apply earlier.
AMCAS opens early May. Submissions open late May / early June.
Med schools use rolling admissions — applications are reviewed in the order received.

T H E T I M I N G R U L E S
• Submit AMCAS within the first two weeks of being open.
• Pre-write secondaries before they arrive & return within 7–14 days.
• If you can't be ready by June, wait a cycle. A late application is worse than a delayed application. By December, many 
schools have already filled nearly 80% of their seats through rolling acceptances.

LONG SHOT Significantly above your stats and/or mission/geographic fit. Apply because the upside is real,

REACH Above your stats but inside a plausible range; things like mission fit can elevate you.
TARGET Your stats land squarely inside the school's median ranges; mission fit aligns.
ATTAINABLE Your stats sit well above median and you're a strong demographic/mission match.

MD vs DO — what the data shows, and how to think about the choice.What we agree on
Before we get to the data, the foundation.
Both are fully licensed physicians
MDs and DOs can prescribe medications, perform surgery, and practice in any specialty across all 50 states.
Both pass the same state boards
Identical licensing requirements once you're a practicing physician.
The DO path produces excellent doctors
Roughly 11% of practicing U.S. physicians hold a DO. Many are exceptional. The degree doesn't determine the doctor.
Patient outcomes don’t differ by degree
Research comparing MD and DO clinical outcomes consistently finds no meaningful differences in patient care quality.Start with admissions

Source: AAMC, via Med School InsidersMyth #2
"MDs treat symptoms with drugs. DOs treat the whole patient."
DOs treat the whole person. MDs only treat the disease.
It's a positioning statement, not a clinical reality.
Prevention, patient-centered care, lifestyle medicine, behavioral health — these are central to allopathic medicine. The idea that 
MDs only treat once disease appears is a myth.
"Holistic" is an individual physician trait, not a degree.
Plenty of MDs practice deeply patient-centered care. Plenty of DOs are highly procedural. The label on the wall doesn't predict 
how a doctor thinks.
The structural difference is OMM, not philosophy.
What actually differs in the DO curriculum is 300–500 additional hours of Osteopathic Manipulative Medicine on top of the same 
foundational training. Whether that's used in practice — next slide.Myth #3

Sources: Journal of Osteopathic Medicine (2020, AOA survey n≈10,000); PLOS ONE / JOM Mayo Clinic study (2025); Cochrane reviews on OMMThe hidden tax: USMLE vs COMLEX
What residency program directors actually do with licensing exams
Both exams qualify you for medical licensure. That's where the equivalence ends.
M D T R A C K
Takes USMLE Step 1, Step 2 CK, Step 3.
One licensing pathway. One set of scores program directors 
are intimately familiar with.
No additional exam burden for any specialty.
D O T R A C K
Required to take COMLEX Level 1, 2, 3.
Program directors widely view COMLEX as less rigorous, so 
many DOs take the USMLE too — particularly anyone 
applying to competitive specialties.
Two licensing pathways = extra cost, time, and pressure on 
top of medical school.
Some programs require a higher COMLEX percentile from DO applicants than they require USMLE from MDs. The bar 
quietly goes up.What residency programs actually see
The hidden structural differences that shape competitiveness — before you ever apply.
Academic medical center affiliation
Most MD programs are built around large academic 
medical centers. Many DO programs aren't — which 
affects the quality and variety of clinical exposure during 
third and fourth year.
Research infrastructure
MD programs typically have more robust research 
ecosystems — funded labs, dedicated research years, 
established publication pipelines. This matters for 
residency applications in nearly every specialty.
Rotation consistency
MD students often complete rotations at one institution 
with continuous faculty. DO rotations are frequently 
dispersed across multiple sites — sometimes states —
with rotating preceptors and uneven mentorship.
Mentor and program network
MD students at academic centers tend to have direct 
access to mentors in competitive specialties. Those 
mentors write letters, make calls, and open doors that 
often aren't available at smaller programs.
Source: Med School Insiders; AAMC institutional data; AACOM rotation site reportingCaribbean medical schools

Match rates and Step pass rates can be quietly engineered. Here's what to look for.
T H E C O M M O N T A C T I C S
Internal pre-USMLE/COMLEX exams. Many international and some smaller schools require students to pass an internal 
NBME-style exam before they're allowed to sit for boards. Students who would fail are filtered out — and never counted in 
the school's published pass rate.
Match rate framing. A 95% "match rate" can include SOAP placements, preliminary spots, and transitional years. Ask for the 
NRMP main match rate, broken out by categorical vs preliminary.
Selective denominators. Some schools only report rates among "eligible students" — excluding those who dropped, took 
longer, or didn't attempt boards.
Q U E S T I O N S T O A S K A N Y M E D I C A L S C H O O L — M D , D O , O R C A R I B B E A N
• What's your attrition rate over the last 5 years?
• What's your first-attempt Step 1 / Step 2 / COMLEX pass rate — without internal pre-exam filtering?
• What's your NRMP main match rate, excluding SOAP and transitional years?
• How many of your grads matched competitive specialties in each of the last 3 cycles?
• What's your accreditation status — full LCME / COCA / CAAM-HP, or candidacy?Six MD vs DO misconceptions, summarized
