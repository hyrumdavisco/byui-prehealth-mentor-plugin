Intro to Research
Schools desire applicants who understand scientific literature, know how research is performed, and can distinguish credible research. Any research, as long as it is hypothesis-based and utilizes the scientific method can build your experience. As you do research, make sure you understand the hypothesis, the procedure, and your responsibility in the project. Participate in research through faculty-directed work, Idaho INBRE, the UC-Davis internship, SPRI,  or other internships nationwide.
Competitive applicants complete at least 1 experience and gain 100+ hours.  Publishing or having a poster presentation is highly beneficial.
Scientific research promotes the attainment of knowledge that answers questions about the world we live in, and specifically for the health professions, provides the understanding necessary to help us live longer and healthier lives. For these reasons, medical personnel need to be able to read and understand scientific literature. They must also communicate effectively and possess the skills to express their thoughts and ideas. Undergraduate courses that demand active involvement and activities, such as independent lab research, and that will foster the attributes required by health professionals today are strongly recommended by professional schools because they will help students commit to becoming lifelong learners. 
In their list of academic requirements for admission to medical school, the University of Utah has defined research as, “involvement in a scholarly or scientific hypothesis investigation that is supervised by an individual with verifiable research credentials.” They go on further to state that research may be done in any discipline, and at any site. It may be part of a class where a grade is received. In all cases, the research must involve the testing of a hypothesis.
In addition to developing critical thinking skills, lab research provides a close interaction with a mentor. This mentor can write a thorough letter of recommendation that can be helpful for the applicant.
BYU-Idaho is supportive of undergraduate research experiences and has several programs to help students fulfill this requirement! Some BYUI professors are currently running independent research labs. In each case, a student may contact the lab mentor to inquire if there are any openings for students during a specific semester. Some of this research is part of a college course, in other cases, students are volunteers or may earn BIO 499R college credit, or even be paid for research. You can also reach out to the Director of Research for help getting connected with research opportunities.


Tips for Success in Research
Principles for Undergraduate Research


First Impression
First impressions are key in the field of research. Make sure to arrive promptly on time every time all the time. Be prepared and know where and when you need to report the first day. You don’t want to be lost in a large lab facility on your first day. Arrive and greet your supervisor with “Good morning, nice to meet you Mr./Mrs./Dr./Professor________"(use whatever title is most appropriate).
Dress
You need to do your research on what is the approved attire in the facility you will be working in. It is always a good rule of thumb to dress the same or one more formal attire compared to the normal daily dress attire at the facility.


Open Mind
As you participate in lab research it is key to always keep an open mind. Look for what interests you and what you can see you may enjoy more of. Realize you may not enjoy all aspects of a project you are involved in.


Take the Initiative
This is your research experience, take the initiative and seek out opportunities, extra duties, more areas to learn and grow your knowledge in. Don’t count the minutes until you can leave for the day and enjoy your lab research and fully dive into the project and apply yourself.


Work Hard
Treat all the time spend on site as precious and work hard. You are a guest, and your labor is very much appreciated by coworkers. Contribute to the workload and be a team player. Also, follow all the rules, especially safety rules!


Ask Questions
Asking good questions shows your desire to understand. This is a great attribute to facilitate as you are in the learning process and understanding what the lab research project entails.


Listen
Listen to your supervisors and pay attention to what they are explaining to you. They are entrusting you with their equipment and facility and wish to have you work independently as a team player. They are placing trust in you that you will take in everything and apply it as you grown in your research expertise knowledge.
Know Your Lab Instructors
Get to know your instructors on a personal level. Take time before, during, or after hours to sit down and get to know your instructors and team members. Know their passions, family, education history, and goals in life. This will develop relationships that will last throughout the research project and beyond.


Lab Notebook
It is always a great practice to keep a Lab Notebook. You want to keep a record of every thought, step, procedure, mistake, hypothesis, and mistake made during the entire research experience. Your memory will serve you well and you keep a detail-oriented journal with everything entailed from lab experience. This notebook should be able to instruct a person how to repeat the lab experiment from start to finish and attain similar results.


Thank You Letter
Upon completion of your research, it is always a professional gesture to sit down and write a personal thank you letter stating your gratitude, what you have learned, how it changed you, and list personal experiences that touched you the most. Print and sign with pen at the bottom and personally hand it to your lead supervisor if possible. As you pursue your undergrad research opportunities, I hope this tutorial will better help you experience the great satisfaction of performing research and contributing to the world of medical research.

UC-Davis Research Internship Program
This program sends current students to live in Davis, California for one semester where they will perform full-time lab research at the UC-Davis campus or Shriner’s Hospital in Sacramento, CA. These internships are unpaid; however, they are sought after by students who want an immersive experience in a biology/neuroscience research lab. This program is open during all 3 BYU-Idaho tracks. Contact Holden Higginbotham for more information.
Idaho INBRE Biomedical Research Program
This federally-funded program sends students to Idaho research university labs for 10 weeks of full-time (40 hours per week) lab research. Universities/Labs participating in this program include: Idaho State University, Boise State University, Boise VA Medical Center, Northwest Nazarene University, the College of Idaho, and the University of Idaho. This program is open during all 3 BYU-Idaho tracks. The current stipend is $4,000 for one semester. Contact Cody Diehl for more information.
SPRI
Summer Pre-medical Research Internship
Application Period: Nov 16 - Dec 7
Application Materials: Cover Letter, Resume, Unofficial Transcript, and if selected, 1-2 letter of recommendation.
Ways to stand out: take lab classes and have prior research experience, develop relationships with professors and professional mentors, develop a professional plan for your future career and goals as they relate to medicine and understand how research might play a role in that plan.
